Counterfactual testing — DGR endorsement event study

Charities that gained DGR endorsement grew donations ~30% in real terms — and the timing shows it’s the endorsement, not the economy

What we did

  • Randomisation isn’t possible — tax status can’t be assigned experimentally. Best feasible design: an event study on charities that actually switched status, each charity its own control.
  • 646 established charities gained DGR endorsement FY2020–23 (ACNC filings × ABR endorsement dates, 2019–2024). Start-ups and ancillary funds excluded.
  • Donations compared before vs after each charity’s own endorsement year, CPI-deflated; median across charities (robust to a heavily right-skewed distribution).
  • Staggered adoption is the counterfactual test: four cohorts endorsed in four different years. A macro shock lifts every cohort in the same calendar year; a DGR effect lifts each cohort at its own endorsement year — which is what we observe.

The timing evidence

Each cohort indexed to its own pre-endorsement median = 100 (grey = before, teal = after, band = endorsement FY). The step-up follows the endorsement year around the calendar.

Robustness: pre-endorsement trend is flat (median real drift from year −2 to −1: 0.0%); guarding against application-period ramp-up moves the estimate 0.3pp. Placebo: never-DGR and always-DGR cohorts show ~0% real median growth over the same window. The effect concentrates in charities with small donation bases (<$10k/yr) — the same profile as the ~17,900 charities that would gain DGR under the PC reform (median donations ~$4.7k/yr). Backup — the growth itself, one line per charity + speaker notes

  • Pseudo-counterfactual, stated honestly: no randomisation, so selection is possible — charities might apply when they expect growth. That is why the anticipation check matters: if applications were timed to expected growth, donations would rise before endorsement. They don’t — the pre-trend is flat.
  • Median, not mean: the growth distribution is heavily right-skewed; the mean would be indefensibly large. +30% is the conservative summary — and 42% of charities fell, which is included in it.
  • Convergent validity: price-elasticity theory at ε = −1 and a 32% marginal rate predicts a +47% ceiling — exactly the nominal median. The real +30% implies ε ≈ −0.6, in line with the giving literature.
  • Mechanism fingerprint: the effect appears only where theory says it should — small-base fundraisers, not $100k+ charities with established non-donation funding. A trend artefact would move everyone.
