Replicating each step in Excel

Setup (do once)

  1. Open dgr_gap_analysis.csv. Note it may be slow — it has ~198,000 rows.
  2. Keep only the latest year per charity: filter ais_year to 2024. (Our scripts take each charity's most recent year, which is 2024 for most. Filtering to 2024 gets you very close.)
  3. Turn the data into a Table (Ctrl+T).

Step 1 — Who's locked out (cohort profiles)

  1. Filter out blank target_subtype (these rows are charities not in any campaign group).
  2. Insert a PivotTable. Rows: target_subtype. Columns: has_dgr. Values: count of abn.
  3. This gives group sizes and how many already have DGR. The % without DGR is the "newly eligible" share — e.g. ~74% of neighbourhood houses have no DGR.
  4. For the money profile: filter has_dgr = FALSE, then in the pivot use median of total_gross_income and donations_and_bequests (Excel pivots don't do median — use =MEDIAN(IF(...)) array formulas on the filtered table, or just sort and eyeball the middle value).
  5. For the state spread: Rows: target_subtype, Columns: state, Values: count.

Step 2 — Layer 1 pool access

Nothing to compute — open reform_scenarios.csv. annual_dollars is the estimated yearly pool access per group under each of three sharing assumptions (basis). Divide annual_dollars by n_newly_eligible for dollars per charity.

Step 3 — The donations gap (DGR vs non-DGR)

  1. Back in the 2024-filtered table, filter charity_size = "Small" and donations_and_bequests > 0.
  2. Filter one purpose column at a time to "Y" — e.g. advancing_social_or_public_welfare for neighbourhood houses.
  3. Compare the median donations_and_bequests for has_dgr = TRUE vs FALSE. You should see DGR charities raise roughly 3–5× more.
  4. Also compare the share with any donations: count rows with donations > 0 divided by all rows, split by has_dgr. DGR charities are 20–35 percentage points more likely to receive anything at all.

Step 4 — Who might lose

Open dgr_incumbent_exposure.csv. Each row shows, for one group and size band: how many existing DGR charities compete in that donor market (n_incumbents), their combined donations (total_donations), and how many rely on donations for over half their income (n_highly_exposed). Compare total_donations to the group's annual_dollars from Step 2 to see the redistribution is small — around 1% or less of the incumbent market in most groups.

Things to know
  • Medians are used everywhere instead of averages because a few giant charities distort averages.
  • donation_dependence = donations ÷ total income (already computed for you).
  • The gap in Step 3 shows association, not proof of cause — charities with DGR may differ in other ways too.
